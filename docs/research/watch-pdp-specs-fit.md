# How watch PDPs present specs and fit

Research for [#11](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/11). Pages were read on 2026-10-09. It feeds [#19 Design the spec table](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/19) and [#20 Design the fit guidance](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/20).

## Short answer

1. The best spec tables are short and in one column. They put the size figures in their own group first: case width, lug-to-lug, thickness, lug width and the wrist range the bracelet fits. Each figure has a clear label and a unit.
2. Brands nearer FOSLN's price (Timex, Seiko USA) usually leave out lug-to-lug. Their buyers ask "will it fit my wrist?" and "can I take links out?", not "how long are the lugs?". Enthusiast brands (Lorier, Baltic, Nomos) list lug-to-lug every time.
3. Fit help works best in layers. Use on-wrist photos with the model's wrist size in the caption, the bracelet's wrist range next to Add to cart, a preview at true scale (on screen or printable), and clear steps for resizing the bracelet.
4. Copy: grouped dimensions, inches and mm together, plain-English explanations of jargon, the wrist range up front, and a resizing tool or steps. Avoid: calling a square case's size a "diameter", single-number sizes for non-round cases, fields that differ from one product to the next, and sizers that can only draw a circle.
5. A square case (CADRE) needs two numbers, width × length, with each one labelled. Square cases tend to look bigger than a round case with the same mm figure. Any size preview has to show the real square shape.

### Order and format

- **Grouping works.** Lorier splits its list into "Specifications" and a separate "Dimensions" block. Nomos and Seiko both group the case figures under a "Dimensions" or "CASE DIMENSIONS" heading. Baymard found that 23% of sites don't group specs into sections at all ([Baymard, 2018](https://baymard.com/research-articles/spec-sheet-scannability)).
- **Short sheets don't need a summary; they need a good order.** Baymard says sheets of about 20 specs or more should be split into titled sub-sections, with "the most sought-after specifications" first. A summary box only pays off once a sheet runs past two screens. Baymard also advises against multi-column layouts, because they make a single product's sheet look like a comparison ([Baymard, 2018](https://baymard.com/research-articles/spec-sheet-scannability)). Both FOSLN Models will have well under 20 rows.
- **Plain-English explanations.** Timex puts a "More info" panel on crystal and water resistance. It turns depth ratings into everyday meaning: "30m Light rain, sweat & splashes", "50m Short swims", "100m Swimming, snorkeling & surfing", "200m Recreational scuba-diving" ([Timex](https://www.timex.com/products/waterbury-classic-40mm-stainless-steel-bracelet-watch-tw2y28500)). Baymard has a guideline titled "Explaining and Defining Specification Labels" ([#840](https://baymard.com/guidelines/840-explaining-and-defining-specification-labels)). Its contents are paywalled and weren't read.
- **Labels matter for thickness.** Lorier and Baltic both say whether the crystal is included ("+ 1.6mm dome crystal", "10.7mm without glass"). Timex's own size guide defines case thickness as running "from the top center of the watch's crystal to the middle of the case back" ([Timex size guide](https://www.timex.com/blogs/the-timex-blog/watch-size-guide)). Without that note, two thickness figures can't be compared.
- **Units.** Every brand gives the case in mm. For the wrist, Timex gives inches and mm, Lorier inches only, Baltic cm only, and Nomos cm and inches ("18 cm (7.1")"). Baymard's sizing research recommends giving both systems, because few shoppers can convert in their head ([Baymard, 2022](https://baymard.com/blog/apparel-size-information)). That research is about apparel, so it applies here by analogy.
- **Consistency across products.** Seiko USA's SUR360 page has no lug width and no length, while SSK009 has both ([SUR360](https://seikousa.com/products/sur360), [SSK009](https://seikousa.com/products/ssk009)). Cartier gives two different thickness figures on one page (8.82 mm and 8.83 mm) ([Cartier](https://www.cartier.com/en-us/watches/collections/santos-de-cartier/santos-de-cartier-watch-CRWSSA0029.html)). Baymard's benchmark found that 30% of sites have "severe issues with inconsistent labels, structure, and units of measure" across products of the same type ([Baymard benchmark](https://baymard.com/product-page/benchmark/page-designs/product-spec-sheet/)). It also found that 52% don't clean up vendor-supplied data ([Baymard, 2018](https://baymard.com/research-articles/spec-sheet-scannability)). This matters for FOSLN, whose figures start on the Supplier's sheets ([#16](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/16)).

### Which measurements buyers rely on

There is no published study that ranks which watch measurements shoppers use. The evidence below is indirect.

- **Mass-market buyers ask about wrist fit and links.** Of 60 Timex steel-bracelet PDPs fetched on 2026-10-09, 28 had customer Q&A, with 56 distinct questions in total. 13 of those 56 were about whether the bracelet would fit a given wrist, extra links, or removing links. Seven asked for extra links or a longer bracelet ("I have a 9 inch wrist, is this watch impossible or do you have links????", "My wrist is 8.5 inches. Do you have longer bands available?"). Two came from people with small wrists ("Can the bracelet size down to 5.8"?"). Three asked about resizing ("Can I adjust the strap myself without taking it to the jeweler?"). One asked about the unit ("8.1 inches, What does the .1 stand for on a measuring tape?"). None asked about lug-to-lug. Timex's replies repeat "we do not sell extra links for our watches" and point buyers to a local jeweler ([Deepwater Arctic](https://www.timex.com/products/deepwater-arctic-40-5mm-stainless-steel-bracelet-watch-tw2y64700), [Q Timex Falcon Eye](https://www.timex.com/products/q-timex-falcon-eye-chronograph-40mm-stainless-steel-bracelet-watch-tw2w33700), [Waterbury Sub-Second](https://www.timex.com/products/waterbury-classic-sub-second-39mm-stainless-steel-bracelet-watch-tw2y91800)). This is a small, self-selected sample. The question-matching was done by script and checked by hand.
- **Jargon gets misread.** One Timex buyer asked for rubber straps to fit "the 12mm lug to lug". The page lists "Band / Lug Width 12 mm", so they meant lug width ([Timex Automatic E-Line 38mm](https://www.timex.com/products/timex-automatic-e-line-38mm-stainless-steel-bracelet-watch-tw6a00500)). This one example supports explaining these terms when they appear.
- **Enthusiast brands treat lug-to-lug as essential.** Lorier, Baltic and Nomos all list it next to the diameter. Watch retailers and brands give the same reason: lug-to-lug sets how much of the wrist the watch covers. Farer calls it "very important because it determines how much of the width of your wrist a watch fills" ([Farer](https://eur.farer.com/blogs/journal/how-different-watch-cases-wear)). Wristler says that ideally "the lugs stay within the top surface of your wrist" ([Wristler, 2026](https://wristler.eu/insights/watch-size-and-wrist)). Both are rules of thumb from sellers, not measured research.
- **Shoppers judge size from photos.** Baymard found that 42% of users try to judge a product's size from its images, and 56% of test subjects started on a new product page by looking at the images ([Baymard, 2017](https://baymard.com/blog/in-scale-product-images)).

## 2. Fit guidance

### Lug-to-lug vs wrist size

The two measurements answer different questions:

- **Wrist circumference** tells the buyer whether the bracelet will close (Timex "150 - 205mm (5.9 - 8.1in)", Lorier "fits up to 8-inch wrists", Baltic "up to 20.5cm"). It also drives the rough case-size advice. Timex says: "Small wrists (<6") suit 28–36mm, average (6–7") fit 36–42mm, and larger wrists (7"+) can wear 42mm+" ([Timex](https://www.timex.com/products/waterbury-classic-40mm-stainless-steel-bracelet-watch-tw2y28500)).
- **Lug-to-lug against the width of the top of the wrist** tells the buyer whether the watch will overhang ([Wristler](https://wristler.eu/insights/watch-size-and-wrist), [Farer](https://eur.farer.com/blogs/journal/how-different-watch-cases-wear)).
- The brands don't agree on which wrist measurement to ask for. Timex asks for circumference and shows buttons from 5.5 to 8 in. Seiko USA asks buyers to "measure the inches from one side of your wrist to the other", and its buttons are wrist widths: 2, 2.2 and 2.4 in on a men's model, 1.6, 1.8 and 2 in on a ladies' model ([SSK009](https://seikousa.com/products/ssk009), [SUR438](https://seikousa.com/products/sur438)). Most people know their circumference, if they know anything, so a width-based tool has to teach the buyer how to measure.

### On-wrist imagery

- Baymard recommends at least one "In Scale" image on every product. For things people wear, it says "the item should be shown on a human model", and it gives "watches on wrists" as its example. 28% of the large sites it checked had no in-scale image at all ([Baymard, 2017](https://baymard.com/blog/in-scale-product-images)).
- The strongest version captions each wrist shot with the model's wrist size. Nomos does this: "Lucas has a wrist circumference of 18 cm (7.1") and a strap length of S", and "Fabian has a wrist circumference of 18.5 cm (7.3") and a strap length of M" ([Nomos](https://nomos-glashuette.com/en-us/tangente/tangente-38-164)). Lorier: "Modeled on a 7-inch wrist" ([Lorier](https://lorierwatches.com/products/watch-falcon-black-nodate-bracelet-325080)). Baymard found the same in apparel: giving the model's measurements and the size they wear "gives users additional context" ([Baymard, 2022](https://baymard.com/blog/apparel-size-information)).
- Showing two different wrist sizes, as Nomos does, lets more buyers find one close to their own.

### On-screen sizers

- **Timex "Try It On"** (at $144.50): the buyer picks a wrist circumference (5.5 to 8 in), the panel states the bracelet range, and it includes "How to measure your wrist size" and "How to choose the right case size" ([Timex](https://www.timex.com/products/waterbury-classic-40mm-stainless-steel-bracelet-watch-tw2y28500)). In the page markup, the watch layer is a file named `Size_Size-40.svg` on a stock wrist image. That suggests a generic 40 mm size graphic rather than a photo of the product. This is inferred from the markup; the rendered tool wasn't seen.
- **Seiko USA "On your wrist"** (at $225 to $650): a canvas tool that draws the product image on a wrist picture and offers three wrist widths ([SSK009](https://seikousa.com/products/ssk009)). How it scales the watch wasn't checked.
- **Cartier**: "CLICK HERE TO TRY ON", then "Scan the QR code with your mobile device", which leads to a phone try-on ([Cartier Santos](https://www.cartier.com/en-us/watches/collections/santos-de-cartier/santos-de-cartier-watch-CRWSSA0029.html)). The phone experience wasn't tested.
- So two brands near FOSLN's price already offer an on-screen sizer. It isn't only a luxury feature.

### Printable sizers

These do two different jobs:

- **Measuring the wrist.** Tissot's PDF is a paper ruler with a watch head drawn on it: "Print the PDF guide and cut the measuring tool", "Make sure to print at 100%!", wrap it round the wrist, read off cm or inches, then "select your wrist size on TissotWatches.com" ([Tissot PDF](https://www.tissotwatches.com/on/demandware.static/-/Library-Sites-Tissot-SharedLibrary/default/dw2e19caf4/3-STANDALONE-PAGES/2-CUSTOMER-SERVICE/SIZE-GUIDES/PDF-SIZE-GUIDE/PDF/how-to-use-our-print-size-tool-en-uk-ie.pdf)). Apple's version adds a scale check: "To make sure you printed at 100%, place a credit or ID card in the box below. If it's an exact fit, you're good to go" ([Apple PDF](https://www.apple.com/shop/Catalog/US/Images/bxxd/size-guide_US.pdf)). Tissot's PDF has no such check.
- **Previewing the watch at true size.** Brew offers "a 1:1 scale printable template... print at 100% scale, and place it on your wrist." It says this "shows the true case size and lug-to-lug proportions beyond just the millimeter measurement" ([Brew Metric Lite FAQ](https://www.brew-watches.com/products/metric-lite-silver-and-black)). Its print steps: "Print at 100% scale (select "Actual Size" or "Do not scale")" and "Ensure "Fit to page" is turned OFF" ([Brew 1:1 Size Guide, Feb 2026](https://www.brew-watches.com/blogs/design-timing-and-everyday-ritual/brew-1-1-size-guide)). Brew's case is not round, which makes this the closest example to CADRE.
- Nomos has no printable sizer. Its FAQ suggests trying the watch on at home with care, within the 14-day return window ([Nomos FAQ](https://nomos-glashuette.com/en/store/faq/ordering)).

## 3. What changes for a square case (CADRE)

- **Two numbers, each labelled.** Cartier lists "DIMENSIONS 41.9 mm x 35.1 mm" and, in the description, "Case width: 35.1 mm". Its small Tank Must reads "29.5 mm x 22.0 mm" ([Santos](https://www.cartier.com/en-us/watches/collections/santos-de-cartier/santos-de-cartier-watch-CRWSSA0029.html), [Tank Must](https://www.cartier.com/en-us/watches/collections/tank/tank-must-de-cartier-watch-CRWSTA0107.html)). The longer figure comes first, but the Specifications row doesn't say which is which. Casio gives square cases as length × width × height, for example A158WA-1 at 36.8 × 33.2 × 8.2 mm ([Casio](https://www.casio.com/intl/watches/casio/product.A158WA-1/); seen only in search snippets).
- **What to avoid.** Brew labels its cushion case "CASE DIAMETER: 36MM x 41.5MM": a round-case word, with no hint of which number is width ([Brew Metric](https://www.brew-watches.com/products/brew-metric-silver)). Timex's tonneau watch gives a single "Case Diameter 42 mm", so the length is unknown ([Timex Tonneau](https://www.timex.com/products/timex-legacy-tonneau-chronograph-42mm-synthetic-rubber-strap-watch-tw2w64500)).
- **Lug-to-lug may be the same as the length.** On a square case with short or built-in lugs, the overall length often is the lug-to-lug. Cartier doesn't say whether its length includes the lugs, so this couldn't be confirmed from its pages. CADRE's spec should say which it is.
- **Square cases look bigger.** Wristler: square or rectangular watches "usually look bigger than round watches with the same millimetre figure". It adds that "the width matters more than the length", and "with square models, 2 to 4 mm less is often the better choice". It also says its wrist-to-case-size chart applies "mainly to round watches" ([Wristler, 2026](https://wristler.eu/insights/watch-size-and-wrist)). Farer says cushion cases "wear larger than their dimensions would suggest", and that for long cases "the lug-to-lug distance is the key measurement" ([Farer](https://eur.farer.com/blogs/journal/how-different-watch-cases-wear)). These are seller rules of thumb, not measurements, and other sellers dispute how big the effect is.
- **Size charts and sizers assume a circle.** Timex's case-size advice and size graphic are both built on diameter (see section 2). Reused for CADRE, they would understate how much wrist it covers. A true-scale preview for CADRE has to draw the real width, length and corners. Brew's 1:1 template is one working example.
- **Round case (ACIER).** The usual set works: diameter, lug-to-lug, thickness, lug width.
- **Same table for both Models.** Baymard's consistency finding argues for identical rows and order on both Models. Only the case-size row changes: "Diameter" for ACIER, "Width × length" for CADRE ([Baymard benchmark](https://baymard.com/product-page/benchmark/page-designs/product-spec-sheet/)).

## 4. Which way this points (for the design tickets, not decisions)

**For [#19 spec table](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/19):**

- One column, with a "Size and fit" or "Dimensions" group at the top: case size, lug-to-lug, thickness (saying whether the crystal is included), lug width, and the wrist range in inches with mm. The rest follows: case and crystal, movement and battery, bracelet and clasp, water resistance (in plain English), warranty.
- At fewer than 20 rows, a sensible order matters more than a summary box (Baymard).
- Short explanations for the terms buyers misread (lug-to-lug, lug width, mineral vs sapphire, water resistance).
- Identical labels and units on ACIER and CADRE, and figures cleaned up from the Supplier's sheets, not copied as they are.
- Trade-off: lug-to-lug is standard for enthusiasts, but Timex and Seiko USA mostly leave it out. Including it costs one row and helps the buyers who look for it. Leaving it out loses nothing for most buyers at the $97 price point, but it makes the table look less complete next to the microbrands.

**For [#20 fit guidance](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/20):**

- The cheapest, best-supported option is on-wrist photos of each Model with the wrist size in the caption, ideally on two wrist sizes ([#17](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/17) will show what imagery exists).
- Next comes the bracelet's wrist range, placed near Add to cart. The Timex Q&A shows this is what buyers ask about most, especially people with large wrists.
- A true-scale preview helps CADRE most. The options are a printable 1:1 template with a credit-card scale check (cheap to make, but the buyer has to print it) or an on-screen tool that draws the real case outline (more work to build, and it needs the real width × length to draw).
- Resizing help: either a tool in the box with step-by-step instructions, or pre-sizing from a wrist size taken at checkout. Which one is possible depends on the Supplier (#26) and the bracelet's pin type (#16).
- Avoid wrist sizers that ask for wrist width without teaching the buyer how to measure it. Avoid decimal inches with no fraction or mm next to them, given the "what does the .1 stand for" question.

## Gaps and things not verified

- Tudor and Casio product pages returned HTTP 403 to every fetch. Their figures above come from search-engine snippets of their own pages and should be treated as unverified.
- Hodinkee Shop no longer shows specs. An archived copy couldn't be fetched, so its old format isn't covered.
- Cartier's "Fit Your Bracelet: None / Remove" option and its phone try-on weren't tested beyond the text on the page.
- How Timex's and Seiko's on-screen tools scale the watch was read from page markup, not seen rendered.
- No research was found that measures which watch dimensions shoppers actually use. The Timex Q&A count (13 of 56 questions) comes from a small, self-selected sample.
- The Baymard sizing findings come from apparel research. Applying them to watches is an analogy, not a tested result. Baymard's full guidelines, including #840, are paywalled.
- The claims that square cases look bigger are rules of thumb from sellers, not measurements.
