# Can FOSLN sell under its brand before registering the business name?

Research for [#46](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/46). All sources checked 11 October 2026 unless a different date is given. This is research, not legal advice. Items marked **Unverified** could not be confirmed from a primary source.

## Answer

Almost certainly not, and there is no reason to try: registering costs A$47 and is usually done the same day, online. *Business Names Registration Act 2011* s 18 makes it a strict-liability offence for an entity to "carr[y] on a business under a name" that isn't registered to it. The only exemption that fits an individual covers the individual's own name, and ASIC reads that as "your first name and surname and nothing else". A store at fosln.com whose checkout header, emails, receipts and card descriptor all say "FOSLN" is trading under that name. No court or ASIC source rules on a brand like this, but every ASIC page says a name other than your own must be registered. ASIC adds that a registered trade mark doesn't remove that need. The domain-name exemption (reg 4 of the Business Names Registration Regulations 2011) only covers the domain form of a name that is *already registered*. "Carrying on" a business also includes setting it up. The maximum penalty is 30 penalty units, which is A$10,920 for an offence on or after 1 July 2026. ASIC's published approach is to write to the business first, and to prosecute only alongside other unlawful conduct or a history of non-compliance. I found no prosecution of a small sole trader under s 18, so the practical risk is low, but it is still an offence. The owner can register before launch: s 23 lets an entity that "intends to carry on a business" apply. The ABN is free and usually issued instantly. The name costs A$47 for 1 year or A$108 for 3 years, the form takes 10–15 minutes, and confirmation comes within 2 business days by card. Both can be done in one browser form. Two side effects to plan for. First, with an ABN, Shopify Payments has to be switched from "Individual" to "Sole trader", which means re-verification. Second, having an ABN does not mean registering for GST. Shopify can show the owner's legal name in place of FOSLN (store name, company name, card descriptor), but that would mean not using the brand at all. For reg 90, "the person's name" is the name of the legal person giving the warranty. For an Individual, that is the owner's full legal name. After registration it is still the owner's legal name, best written as "[legal name] trading as FOSLN". That wording also meets BNRA s 19, which requires the business name on business documents.

## 1. Does trading as "FOSLN" count as carrying on business under that name?

### The offence: s 18 (current text)

*Business Names Registration Act 2011*, Compilation No. 10, compilation date 1 July 2026, Authorised Version C2026C00334 ([FRL](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2011A00126/latest/text); [PDF](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2011A00126/2026-07-01/2026-07-01/text/original/pdf)):

> **18 Offence—carrying on a business under an unregistered business name**
> (1) An entity commits an offence if:
> (a) the entity carries on a business under a name; and
> (b) the name is not registered to the entity as a business name on the Business Names Register.
> Penalty: 30 penalty units.
> (2) Subsection (1) does not apply if:
> (a) the entity is an individual and the name is the individual's name; or
> (b) the entity is a corporation and the name is the corporation's name; or
> (c) the entity is a partnership and the name consists of all of the partners' names; or
> (d) the name is registered to the entity on a notified State/Territory register; or
> (e) an Act of the Commonwealth, a referring/adopting State or an affected Territory, or an instrument made under such an Act, specifies the name as the name of the entity; or
> (f) the entity is a government body; or
> (g) the entity is a notified successor in relation to the name; or
> (h) other circumstances prescribed by the regulations for the purposes of this paragraph apply.
> Note: A defendant bears an evidential burden in relation to the matters in subsection (2) …
> (3) Subsection (1) is an offence of strict liability.

The definitions in s 3 that matter here:

- "**business name** means a name used, or to be used, in relation to one or more businesses."
- "**carrying on a business** includes doing anything in the course of the commencement or termination of the business." So setting up the store and taking pre-orders already count.
- s 4(1): "**Business** means an activity, or series of activities, done: (a) in the form of a profession, a trade, employment, a vocation or a calling; or (b) in the form of an adventure or concern in the nature of trade …". s 4(2) excludes activities for which the entity "would not be entitled to an ABN" (a hobby, for example). A store run for profit is entitled to an ABN, so it is a business whether or not it *has* one ([ASIC RG 235.33–235.36](https://download.asic.gov.au/media/1247075/rg235.pdf), Aug 2013).
- s 16(1)(a) sets out the purpose: "to ensure that if an entity carries on a business under a business name, those who engage or propose to engage with that business can identify the entity and how the entity may be contacted".

### Exemptions that could apply to FOSLN

| Exemption | Applies before registration? |
|---|---|
| s 18(2)(a) own name | **No.** "FOSLN" isn't the owner's name. ASIC: "If you are operating under your own name, you must use your first name and surname and nothing else" ([ASIC, Register a business name](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name)). RG 235.29: "The law does not allow any changes from the business holder's name." |
| s 18(2)(b)–(g) | No. FOSLN isn't a corporation, partnership, government body or notified successor, and has no State register. |
| s 18(2)(h) + Regulations reg 4 (domain names) | **No.** Reg 4(1) covers "the domain name form of a business name that: (a) is registered to the entity" (example: "'fredsfish.com.au' if the business name registered to an entity is 'Fred's Fish'"). Reg 4(2) covers a name made by dropping host names or extensions from a registered domain-name business name. Both need a registered name first ([Business Names Registration Regulations 2011](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2011L02406/latest/text), Compilation No. 4, 11 July 2026, F2026C00753). |
| s 25 | **Not an exemption.** s 25 sets out when a name is *available* to register (not identical or nearly identical to another name, not undesirable, no restricted words). It gives no defence to s 18. |
| A trade mark | **No.** ASIC: "You will still need to register the business name with ASIC even if you have a registered trademark to protect your business name" ([ASIC, Business names vs trade marks](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name/business-names-vs-trademarks)). Registering a name gives no property in it (s 17(2)). Only a trade mark gives exclusive rights. |

### Is a brand on the store "a name" the business is carried on under?

- **No case or ASIC document directly says whether a product or store brand counts.** I found no judgment on the meaning of "carries on a business under a name" in s 18. The Explanatory Memorandum ([C2011B00148EM](https://www.legislation.gov.au/bills/C2011B00148EM_1.pdf), paras 3.2–3.8) says only that the offence covers carrying on business "under a business name that is not registered". It says nothing about brands. **Unverified** beyond these sources.
- **The ASIC guidance is categorical:** "You must register a business name if you run a business in Australia and are not trading under your own name" ([ASIC, Register a business name](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name)). "If you conduct business under a name other than your own, you must register a business name" ([ASIC, Trading names are not registered business names](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-basics/trading-names-are-not-registered-business-names)). business.gov.au says: "It's an offence to trade under an unregistered business name unless it's the same as your own name (for example, Mary Jones)" ([Business names, trading names and legal names](https://business.gov.au/planning/new-businesses/business-names-trading-names-and-legal-names)).
- **My reading (inference):** a retailer that stocks a third-party brand isn't trading under that brand. FOSLN is different, because "FOSLN" would be the name customers deal with: the domain, the checkout header, the email sender, the receipts and the card descriptor. That is a name "used … in relation to" the business (s 3), and it is how a customer identifies whom they're dealing with (s 16(1)(a)). Reg 4 points the same way. It exempts trading under the *domain form* of a registered name, which would be pointless if trading under a domain or brand weren't "carrying on a business under a name". A few branded product labels on an otherwise "[Owner's name]" store would be a closer call. That isn't the FOSLN plan.

## 2. Penalty and enforcement

- **Maximum:** 30 penalty units (s 18(1)). A Commonwealth penalty unit is **A$364 for offences on or after 1 July 2026** (A$330 from 7 Nov 2024 to 30 Jun 2026), so the maximum is **A$10,920** ([ASIC, Fines and penalties](https://asic.gov.au/about-asic/asic-investigations-and-enforcement/fines-and-penalties/)). Strict liability means no intent is needed. The EM (para 3.7) says the defence of reasonable mistake of fact still applies. *(Not knowing the law is not a mistake of fact under Criminal Code s 9.3; I didn't re-read s 9.3.)*
- **The related offence after registration:** s 19 requires "clearly legible business names information" on business documents: 5 penalty units (A$1,820). On "a statement of account (including an invoice)", "a receipt", "an order for goods or services" and "an offer to provide goods or services", that means the name **and the ABN** (s 19(2)). On any other document it means the name (s 19(3)). The own-name exemption (s 19(4)(a)) works the same way as in s 18.
- **No infringement-notice scheme:** the Act has no infringement notices (searched the full compilation), so a penalty would need a prosecution.
- **ASIC's stated practice** ([INFO 200, Unregistered business names](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/contact-us/reporting-misconduct-to-asic/unregistered-business-names), issued April 2015): "Our aim is to get businesses to comply with their obligations. If we receive information that someone is operating under an unregistered business name, we may write to the business to remind them of their obligations to register the business name and, in some cases, we may take further regulatory action. Generally, we will only take action if we have concerns about other unlawful conduct, or when the business has a history of failing to meet their obligations under the law."
- **Prosecutions:** I found no reported prosecution of a small sole trader under s 18. **Unverified.** I didn't search ASIC's enforcement reports or AustLII exhaustively.
- **Other exposure (inference):** a business name is public information that tells consumers who is behind the brand (s 16). Missing it isn't misleading conduct in itself. But a chargeback or ACL dispute is easier to handle when the card descriptor and the ABN Lookup record both say FOSLN.

## 3. Registering "FOSLN" before launch

- **Allowed:** s 23(1): "An entity that intends to carry on a business under a name may lodge with ASIC an application for the name to be registered to the entity as a business name." The application needs an ABN, or "if an application for an ABN is pending, … the reference number for the ABN application" (s 23(2)(a)(ii)). ASIC must register the name once the entity has an ABN, the fee is paid, the name is available and the entity isn't disqualified (s 24(1)).
- **ABN first, also before launch:** the ABR checks that you "commenced, or took steps to commence, your business or enterprise from the start date provided" ([ABR, Applying for an ABN](https://www.abr.gov.au/business-super-funds-charities/applying-abn), last modified 11 Jun 2026, read via the [Wayback copy of 28 Sep 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260928052538/https://www.abr.gov.au/business-super-funds-charities/applying-abn) because the live site refused this environment). Setting up the store counts as taking steps to start. An ABN is free and doesn't trigger GST, which starts at A$75,000 turnover (see [#29's note](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/blob/research/selling-au-unregistered/docs/research/selling-au-unregistered.md)).
- **Don't register too early:** ASIC may cancel a name if the entity hasn't carried on business under it for the preceding 3 months, after 28 days' notice (s 47). Because "carrying on" includes the commencement steps, registering a few weeks before launch is fine.
- **Cost:** **A$47 for 1 year or A$108 for 3 years** ([business.gov.au, Register your business name](https://business.gov.au/registrations/register-your-business-name)). The fee rose from A$45 and A$104 on 1 July 2026 ([business.gov.au, Changes for businesses from 1 July 2026](https://business.gov.au/news/changes-for-businesses-from-1-july-2026)). ASIC's own pages showed fee placeholders ("{BZA}") instead of amounts. The fee must be paid within 10 business days of approval, or the application is refused ([ASIC, How to register a business name](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name/how-to-register-a-business-name-with-asic)).
- **Time, ABN:** "you'll receive your 11-digit ABN immediately" if the application succeeds. If you get a reference number, "We aim to review your application within 20 business days" ([ABR](https://web.archive.org/web/20260928052538/https://www.abr.gov.au/business-super-funds-charities/applying-abn)).
- **Time, business name:** "around 10 to 15 minutes to apply … online". Confirmation comes within "2 business days for payment by credit card" or "5 business days for payment by BPAY, EFT or bank transfer" (business.gov.au, above). ASIC says the name "is registered when you have submitted your application and payment has cleared. This can take up to 24 hours", and elsewhere "within 48 hours". It also says "You can start using your business name when it appears on the business names register" ([ASIC, Register a business name](https://www.asic.gov.au/for-business-and-companies/business-names/register-a-business-name)).
- **Possible delay for a made-up word:** ASIC's availability check can return amber for "words that are made up, words that have an unusual spelling", and "The name will be put in a queue for a manual assessment" (same ASIC page). "FOSLN" is a made-up word. ABN Lookup on 11 Oct 2026 found no "FOSLN" name. Its only fuzzy match was "foslyn consulting" (WA, business name), which doesn't look identical or nearly identical. I didn't run ASIC's traffic-light check. **Unverified.** Run the check before relying on a same-day registration.
- **No terminal needed:** the [Business Registration Service](https://register.business.gov.au/) is a browser form: "register here for an Australian Business Number (ABN) … you can register for a business name or taxes at the same time". ASIC Connect is also a web form. **Unverified:** whether the new-business form needs a myID login. The BRS page mentions Digital ID only for tax registrations on an existing ABN. **Note:** on 11 Oct 2026 the BRS showed a notice that "We are currently experiencing an intermittent issue that may affect business name and company registrations."

**Can it show the owner's legal name until registration?** Yes. Every customer-facing name is a merchant setting: the store name, the company name (`legal_business_name`) and the descriptor, which may be the "legal entity name". A store that shows "[Owner's full name]" everywhere would fit the own-name exemption. **But** that means not using "FOSLN" as the trading name at all: not in the checkout header, the email sender, the receipts or the descriptor. A FOSLN logo on a checkout headed with the owner's name would be borderline (inference). A cheaper, cleaner fix is to register before launch.

## 5. Reg 90: "the person's name" on the warranty

*Competition and Consumer Regulations 2010*, Compilation No. 100, 1 September 2026, F2026C00856 ([FRL](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B01420/latest/text)), reg 90(1)(d):

> a warranty against defects must prominently state the following information about the person who gives the warranty;
> (i) the person's name;
> (ii) the person's business address;
> (iii) the person's telephone number;
> (iv) the person's email address (if any);

- **"Person"** means a legal person. *Acts Interpretation Act 1901* s 2C(1) (Compilation No. 39): words such as "person" "include a body politic or corporate as well as an individual". It applies to regulations through *Legislation Act 2003* s 13(1)(a) (not re-read). A business name is not a legal person. Registration gives no property in it (BNRA s 17(2)), and the sole trader stays the entity (BNRA s 5(1)(a)).
- **Individual, before registration:** the warrantor is the owner, so the warranty must give the owner's **full legal name**. The ACCC says "the business's name" ([ACCC, Warranties](https://www.accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/warranties), read via the [Wayback copy of 30 Jul 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260730204837/https://www.accc.gov.au/business/selling-products-and-services/warranties)). For an unregistered individual, that name *is* their own name. Putting only "FOSLN" here would also be using the unregistered name on a business document.
- **Sole trader with "FOSLN" registered:** the person is still the individual, so reg 90 still points to the legal name. BNRA s 19 adds that a business document "connected with carrying on the business under the name" must include the business name. So the safe form is **"[Owner's full legal name] trading as FOSLN"**, with the ABN, which s 19(2) requires on receipts and invoices but not on a warranty card. **Unverified:** no ACCC guidance or case says whether the registered business name alone would satisfy "the person's name". A business name is searchable on the ASIC register, which links it to the holder. The legal name plus "trading as" meets both laws, and nothing more is needed.
- **Practical point:** the ACCC says the mandatory warranty information "must be available with the actual product". The warranty card in the box therefore needs updating when the business name is registered.
