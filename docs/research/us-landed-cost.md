# US landed cost for dropshipped watches

Research for [#9](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/9), part of the fosln.com v1 map ([#4](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/4)). Status as of **9 October 2026**. It reports what the sources say. It is not legal or tax advice.

## Short answer

1. A quartz watch on a steel bracelet falls under **HTS 9102.11**. ACIER is most likely **9102.11.25**. CADRE's line depends on whether its gold frame is real gold plating, and both Models depend on the movement's jewel count. De minimis is gone, so every order pays duty.
2. Duty today on a China-made watch is the normal rate (about 9–10% of value for this product) **+ 7.5%** (Section 301, List 4A) **+ 12.5%** (the new Section 301 forced-labor tariff). That comes to **about 30% of the declared customs value**, plus a CBP fee of $1.38–$2.77 per parcel and whatever the carrier or broker charges. At a $97 declared value that is about **$29–31**. At a $25 Supplier price it is about **$9–10**.
3. Someone has to be the importer and pay CBP before the parcel is released. **DDP**, where the Supplier's line or the carrier pays and the cost is built into the price, is the workable route. Under **DAP** the customer gets the bill plus fees, and US mail no longer collects duty at the door.
4. Supplier express lines quote **5–8 business days** to the US after Dispatch (YunExpress), so order to delivery is roughly **6–9 business days**, plus any customs hold.
5. For the store, the facts point to one **duty-inclusive price**, DDP only, and a delivery promise written as Dispatch plus a business-day range.

## 2. HTS classification

Quartz analog watches are "electrically operated" wrist watches "with mechanical display only", so they fall under subheading **9102.11**. Heading 9101 is only for cases of precious metal, or of metal clad with precious metal. The 8-digit line then turns on three facts ([USITC HTS, chapter 91, 2026 Rev. 20](https://hts.usitc.gov/reststop/file?release=currentRelease&filename=Chapter%2091)):

| Line | Jewels in movement | Bracelet | Case | General (MFN) rate, which applies to China |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **9102.11.25** | none or 1 | base metal (steel) | not gold- or silver-plated | 40¢ each + 8.5% on the case + 14% on the bracelet + 5.3% on the battery |
| 9102.11.10 | none or 1 | base metal | gold- or silver-plated | 44¢ each + 6% on the case + 14% on the bracelet + 5.3% on the battery |
| 9102.11.65 | more than 1 | base metal | not plated | 76¢ each + 8.5% on the case + 14% on the bracelet + 5.3% on the battery |
| 9102.11.50 | more than 1 | base metal | gold- or silver-plated | 80¢ each + 6% on the case + 14% on the bracelet + 5.3% on the battery |

- **ACIER** has a steel case and a steel bracelet, so it is 9102.11.25, or .65 if the movement has more than one jewel.
- **CADRE**'s "gold frame" decides between the plated and unplated lines. A gold-coloured coating with no gold in it is probably not "gold-plated", but I didn't find a ruling on this. The Supplier should say what the finish is, and a broker should confirm the line.
- **Bracelet:** a steel bracelet sold with the watch is classified with the watch, attached or not (chapter 91, Additional U.S. Note 2). The battery is too (Note 3).
- **Component values:** the rate is compound, so the entry has to split the declared value into movement, case, bracelet and battery (suffixes 10/20/30/40), and the parts must add up to the total (chapter 91, Statistical Note 1). The bracelet carries the highest rate (14%), so how the value is split moves the result.
- **Marking:** watch movements and cases "shall not be permitted to be entered" unless they are marked indelibly. The movement needs the country of manufacture, the maker's or purchaser's name and the jewel count in words. The caseback needs the country of manufacture and the maker's or purchaser's name (chapter 91, Additional U.S. Note 4). This affects caseback design and what the Supplier engraves.
- **Section 232:** neither 9102 nor 9113 appears in the steel or aluminium derivative lists in the current chapter 99. I searched the 2026 Rev. 20 PDF, generated 28 Sep 2026.

**Rule of thumb:** duty ≈ **30% of declared value** (about 28–32% for the lines and value split used here), plus a few cents of specific duty. The two Section 301 layers alone are a fixed 20%.

US customs value excludes international freight and insurance ([19 U.S.C. 1401a(b)(4)(A)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1401a)), so the delivery charge itself isn't dutiable as long as it's identified separately. There's no federal VAT. State sales tax is a separate checkout question, already parked on the map under "US payments and tax configuration".

### Worked examples

Assumptions: 9102.11.25. The declared value is split 20% movement, 40% case, 38% bracelet and 2% battery; this split is an assumption, so get the real one from the Supplier. Rates as of 9 Oct 2026.

| Declared value | Normal duty | +7.5% (301 List 4A) | +12.5% (301 forced labor) | **Total duty** | + courier hub fee $1.38 | or + informal MPF $2.77 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $25 (an illustrative Supplier price) | $2.61 | $1.88 | $3.12 | **$7.61** (30.4%) | $8.99 | $10.38 |
| $50 | $4.81 | $3.75 | $6.25 | **$14.81** (29.6%) | $16.19 | $17.58 |
| $97 (retail price) | $8.96 | $7.27 | $12.12 | **$28.36** (29.2%) | $29.74 | $31.13 |

The other lines move these totals by under $1 at $97: 9102.11.65 (more than one jewel) adds $0.36, and 9102.11.10 (gold-plated case) saves about $0.93. The carrier's or DDP line's own fee comes on top of every figure.

### Which value gets declared

Customs value is "the price actually paid or payable for the merchandise when sold for exportation to the United States", minus international freight ([19 U.S.C. 1401a(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1401a)). A dropship order involves two sales, Supplier to FOSLN and FOSLN to customer. Which one sets the value depends on how the order is structured: who buys from whom, who owns the goods in transit, and who is the importer. The gap is large, roughly $7.61 against $28.36 of duty per watch. Undervaluation is a stated CBP enforcement target ([91 FR 37801](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-24/pdf/2026-12669.pdf)), and false entry statements carry penalties under [19 U.S.C. 1592](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/19/1592). This is one for the importer of record's broker to settle. It ties directly to [#26](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/26) (how orders reach the Supplier).

## 5. Who pays: DDP vs DAP

**The legal floor:** an informal entry can only be made by the **owner or purchaser**, or by a **licensed customs broker** they or the consignee designate ([19 CFR 143.26](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-143/subpart-C/section-143.26)). Articles classified in chapter 99, subchapters III and IV (which covers both Section 301 layers), are excluded from informal entry if valued over **$250** ([19 CFR 143.21(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-143/subpart-C/section-143.21)). A single-watch parcel stays under that, so it can use informal entry. CBP has proposed dropping the $250 cap.

- **DDP (delivered duty paid):** "the seller assumes responsibility for any import costs" ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/carriers-and-shipping-terms)). In a dropship setup this means the Supplier's logistics line, or FOSLN's own courier account, files the entry through its broker, pays CBP, and bills FOSLN in the delivery charge. The customer pays nothing more at the door.
- **DAP (delivered at place):** "the customer is responsible for paying any import costs to the shipping carrier", and "using the DAP incoterm might result in additional charges to the customer" ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/carriers-and-shipping-terms)). DHL's US FAQ says duties "are typically the responsibility of the receiver". DHL pays customs on the receiver's behalf and then charges the receiver, unless the shipper chooses to pay all charges when creating the shipment ([DHL US FAQ](https://mydhl.express.dhl/us/en/help-and-support/faqs/duties-and-taxes.html)).

**How it plays out by channel:**

| Channel | What the rules now require | Fit for FOSLN |
|---|---|---|
| **Express courier** (DHL Express, FedEx, UPS) | The courier's broker files an informal entry. Duty is billed to the receiver (DAP) or the shipper (DDP). | Works. DDP needs a shipper-billed account. |
| **Supplier "dedicated line"** (air cargo to the US, then USPS or UPS for the last mile; e.g. YunExpress) | The line's broker files Type 11 entries. A DDP line folds the duty into its per-parcel price. | Works if the line is DDP and says who the importer is. I found **no first-party page** from a Chinese line that sets out its US duty terms, so get this from the Supplier in writing. |
| **Postal mail** (China Post or EMS, delivered by USPS) | Since 24 Jul 2026 the filer must be the owner, the purchaser or a licensed broker, **with a bond**, and must pay CBP monthly via Pay.gov. Foreign posts can no longer act as filers. CBP officers no longer prepare entries, and "duties will not be collected upon delivery" ([91 FR 37801](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-06-24/pdf/2026-12669.pdf); [CBP guidance](https://content.govdelivery.com/attachments/USDHSCBP/2026/07/08/file_attachments/3708650/Updated%20Global%20Guidance%20for%20International%20Mail-7.7.26.pdf)). USPS "does not currently offer the ability to prepay duties and taxes for inbound international mail" ([USPS](https://www.usps.com/international/prepaid-import-duties.htm)). **From 22 Oct 2026** Chinese watches by mail need formal entry (minimum MPF $34.58) or the voluntary Entry Type 13 test. | Poor. The old "pay at the door" mail model no longer exists for these goods. |

The pattern: since de minimis ended, the importer role can't be skipped. Either the Supplier's line or carrier carries it (DDP), or the customer is pulled in at delivery (DAP). Mail is the costliest channel for a Chinese watch from 22 Oct 2026.

**Questions for the Supplier:** which line or carrier they use for US orders; whether it's DDP; who files as importer and through which broker; what value they declare and on what basis; which HTS line and component split; whether the 7.5% and 12.5% Section 301 duties are included in the quote; the movement model and jewel count; whether CADRE's case is actually gold-plated; and whether the caseback and movement carry the required marks.

## 6. What may still change

- **Section 301 China review.** USTR has started the second four-year review of Lists 1–4A. It "will remain in effect, subject to possible further modifications" ([91 FR 64212](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-07/pdf/2026-20510.pdf)). The 7.5% could go up, go down or stay.
- **Forced-labor tariff.** It's new and no end date is stated. Its exemptions have already been revised once: from 31 Jul 2026, patented pharmaceuticals were added to an exemption. I saw that in the notice annex as reproduced by [Global Trade Alert's China extract](https://ricardo-dashboard.s3.eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/reports/forced-labour-section-301-final-action/economies/S301-FL_China.pdf), not in the Federal Register PDF. China is in the 12.5% tier because it has no forced-labor import ban ([91 FR 47318](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-07-28/pdf/2026-15181.pdf)).
- **US–China truce.** Reporting says the one-year "Busan" truce, due to end 10 Nov 2026, was extended to **10 Jan 2027** on 23–27 Sep 2026 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/economy/us-china-extend-trade-truce-trump-rcna599525)). I didn't find this in an official notice; [USTR's 27 Sep release](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/september/ambassador-greer-issues-statement-announcement-recommendations-us-china-board-trade) doesn't state the date. Watches are **not** on the [US Import List](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/US-Public-List.pdf) of Chinese goods proposed for better tariff treatment, so no relief is on the table for them.
- **Other open Section 301 cases.** An investigation into China's Phase One compliance (opened Oct 2025) has no determination that I could find. I saw this only in secondary sources.
- **CBP low-value NPRM (8 Oct 2026, comments due 60 days after publication).** Proposals include filing Type 11 entries on or before arrival, a basic importation bond for every Type 11 and Type 13 entry ($1,000 minimum liquidated damages), a broker acting as importer whenever the consignee isn't the owner or purchaser, and treating unentered mail as abandoned after 15 days ([NPRM](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-20650.pdf)). If adopted, these raise per-parcel costs for lines and couriers. That would most likely reach FOSLN through the DDP price.
- **Mail deadline.** 22 Oct 2026 (above). Entry Type 13 is a test and could change.
- **De minimis litigation.** The CIT upheld the suspension on 13 Aug 2026, and an appeal is possible. Even a win wouldn't help for long, because the statutory repeal takes effect on 1 Jul 2027.
- **Fees.** CBP user fees are adjusted for inflation each 1 October.

Customs holds or missing entry data add time. The NPRM's proposed "entry on or before arrival" rule would hold up parcels whose data is late.

## 8. Effect on the store

These are facts and the direction they point. The decisions belong to [#24](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/24) (prices), [#25](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/25) (shipping and returns), [#26](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/26) (Supplier orders) and [#21](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/21) (platform).

**Price shown**
- Per-watch landed cost ≈ Supplier price + delivery charge + **about 30% of declared value** + $1.38–$2.77 CBP fee + the line's or broker's fee. At $97 retail that is **$9–31 of import cost per watch**, depending mostly on the declared value.
- Under DDP the customer sees one price with nothing added at the door. Under DAP a $97 watch can bring a bill of roughly $30, plus the courier's fee, at delivery. That points toward a **duty-inclusive price**, with "duties and import fees included" stated plainly.
- 20 points of the rate are Section 301 layers that could change (§6), so it's worth testing the margin at duty rates about 10 points above and below today's.

**Delivery promise**
- The FTC's Mail, Internet, or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule requires "a reasonable basis" for any stated time. Its clock runs on **shipment**, meaning goods "physically placed in the possession of the carrier", which is FOSLN's **Dispatch**. If no time is stated, the seller must expect to send within 30 days. Delays require notice and an offer to cancel. "The person soliciting the order, not the agent fulfilling it, is the seller", so FOSLN is responsible for the Supplier's performance ([FTC business guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/business-guide-ftcs-mail-internet-or-telephone-order-merchandise-rule)).
- That points toward stating two figures, Dispatch time and a delivery range in business days, backed by records of the Supplier's actual times.
- Shop Promise badges are for "qualifying US domestic orders" built on delivery history, so a China-to-US dropship flow is unlikely to qualify ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/shop-promise)). Shopify can show manual delivery dates calculated from fulfillment time plus the transit time the store sets ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/fulfillment/setup/delivery-expectations)).

**Checkout (Shopify as the working assumption)**
- Shopify's built-in duty collection works "only on international shipments", judged by the fulfillment location. It needs HS codes and country of origin, and a **DDP label** from a supported carrier: Canada Post (US only), DHL Express, DHL Express Canada or DHL eCommerce. Otherwise "you can't purchase a label for orders where duties have been collected". A store can offer DDP or DAP for a country, not both. A 0.5% transaction fee applies (temporarily lowered from 0.85% or 1.5%) ([considerations](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/considerations); [charging duties](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/charging-duties); [carriers](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/carriers-and-shipping-terms)).
- In a dropship flow the Supplier buys the label, so this feature doesn't fit easily. The simpler path the facts point to is DDP through the Supplier's line, with the duty built into the product price, and no duty line at checkout. Shopify's US tariff page confirms that duties apply to every US import whatever its value ([Shopify](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/duties-and-import-taxes/understanding-tariffs)).
- Whichever way #25 goes, the shipping policy will need to say who pays import charges. I didn't research whether duty can be recovered when a watch is returned to the Supplier (duty drawback).
