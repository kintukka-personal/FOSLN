# Can a PO Box be the warranty's business address?

Research for [#44](https://github.com/Kintukka2/FOSLN-WATCH/issues/44). All sources checked 11 October 2026 unless a different date is given. This is research, not legal advice.

## Answer

No law, regulator or court says yes or no. Reg 90 asks only for "the person's business address". It defines nothing and says nothing about PO Boxes. Where the same lawmakers wanted to rule PO Boxes out, they said so in words. The ACL's unsolicited-agreement rule (s 79(d)(iv)) says "business address (not being a post box)". Reg 82 in the same Regulations says "address (not being a post office box)". The Explanatory Statement for the regulations that added reg 90 also says that reg 82's plain "address" may be "a post office box, business or workplace address". Reg 90 has no such exclusion, so the wording points to a Private PO Box being allowed. But no ACCC, state or court source confirms this. The ordinary meaning of "business address" could still be read as a place where the business is carried on. A wrong guess is a strict-liability offence, with a maximum of A$10,000 per contravention for an individual. The ACCC and Queensland guidance just say "address" or "business address". A street address that isn't the owner's home costs about A$20–A$29 a month through a virtual mailbox, signed up online. That removes most of the doubt, and the PO Box can stay as the claims and returns address. Getting an ABN and registering "FOSLN" does not change the reg 90 test. It gives FOSLN two privacy facts: the ABR never publishes the business address, and ASIC shows only the suburb, state and postcode for an individual whose place of business is their home.

## 1. What the law says

### Regulation 90 (current compilation)

*Competition and Consumer Regulations 2010*, Compilation No. 100, compilation date 1 September 2026, register ID F2026C00856 ([Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1996B01420/latest/text)). Reg 90(1), verbatim (the semicolon after "warranty" in (d) is in the official text):

> For subsection 102(1) of the Australian Consumer Law, the following requirements are prescribed: …
> (d) a warranty against defects must prominently state the following information about the person who gives the warranty;
> (i) the person's name;
> (ii) the person's business address;
> (iii) the person's telephone number;
> (iv) the person's email address (if any);
> …
> (f) a warranty against defects must set out the procedure for the consumer to claim the warranty including the address to which a claim may be sent;

- Reg 90 doesn't define "business address", and neither does the rest of the Regulations or the ACL dictionary (searched the full compilation text).
- Reg 90(1)(f) is a separate "address to which a claim may be sent". Nothing restricts it, so the PO Box clearly works as the **claims** address.
- Endnotes: reg 90 was added by SLI No 280, 2010, as amended by No 337, 2010, and amended by F2018L00737. The 2018 change added the services wording to (1)(c) and the text in (3)–(4). It did not touch (d).

### Where the law does exclude PO Boxes

- **ACL s 79(d)(iv)**, for unsolicited consumer agreements (door-to-door and phone sales), requires "the supplier's business address (not being a post box) or, if the supplier does not have a business address, the supplier's residential address" (*Competition and Consumer Act 2010* Sch 2, Compilation No. 167, 16 September 2026, [FRL](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/latest/text)). Online sales are not unsolicited consumer agreements, so this rule doesn't apply to FOSLN.
- **Reg 82(c)**, in the same Regulations as reg 90, requires "the supplier's name and address (not being a post office box)". Reg 82(b) asks only for "the dealer's address", with no exclusion.
- **Explanatory Statement** for the *Trade Practices (Australian Consumer Law) Amendment Regulations 2010 (No. 1)*, the instrument that added both reg 82 and reg 90 ([FRL, F2010L03014, PDF](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2010L03014/asmade/2010-11-18/es/original/pdf), PDF pp 8 and 14):
  - On reg 82: "A dealer may provide a post office box, business or workplace address. … A supplier is not permitted to use a post office box address, to ensure that a consumer, or a consumer law enforcement agency, is able to readily contact a supplier…"
  - On reg 90 it says only that the warranty "prominently states the name, business address, telephone number and email address (if any) of the person who gives the warranty". Its stated purpose is that consumers struggle to enforce warranties "if they lack access to basic details, such as the name and address of the supplier". It says the requirements are "similar to those that currently apply under Division 5, Part 3 of the Fair Trading Act 1989 (Qld)". *(I did not check the Queensland predecessor text.)*

**Reading:** the same instrument, made at the same time, says "not being a post office box" in reg 82 and says nothing like it in reg 90. Its own Explanatory Statement treats a plain "address" as including a PO Box. Reg 90 adds the word "business", which s 79 shows can still need an express PO Box exclusion. Together these favour a PO Box being allowed. **This is my inference from the drafting. No authority states it.**

### What a wrong guess costs

- ACL s 102(2) makes it a civil contravention to give a consumer a warranty document that doesn't comply. The maximum penalty for a person who is not a body corporate is A$10,000 per act or omission (s 224(3), item 9).
- ACL s 192 makes the same thing a **strict-liability offence**, also A$10,000 for an individual.

## 4. Does an ABN and the "FOSLN" business name change it?

**The reg 90 test doesn't change.** Reg 90 asks for "the person's business address" whether or not the person has an ABN. What changes:

- **ABR:** the business address, the mailing address for notices and phone numbers are "not publicly available". ABN Lookup shows the state and postcode of the main business address ([ABR, Your ABN details on the ABR](https://abr.gov.au/business-super-funds-charities/applying-abn/your-abn-details-abr), last modified 5 Aug 2024; read via the [Wayback copy of 7 Sep 2026](https://web.archive.org/web/20260907054043/https://www.abr.gov.au/business-super-funds-charities/applying-abn/your-abn-details-abr)).
- **ASIC Business Names Register** ([Registering a business name](https://asic.gov.au/for-business/registering-a-business-name/); [Personal information shown on the registers](https://www.asic.gov.au/online-services/online-services-information-and-support/personal-information-shown-on-the-business-names-and-companies-registers)):
  - **Principal place of business:** "the main place you carry out business. It must be a physical street address in Australia." It is public, but "if your place of business is your home address and you operate as an individual, we will only show your suburb, state or territory and postcode."
  - **Service of documents address:** "must be a physical street address or PO Box in Australia", and it "is always available to the public… You could use a PO Box, or your accountant's address instead."
  - Required by *Business Names Registration Act 2011* s 23(2)(a)(iii)–(iv) (Compilation No. 10, 1 July 2026).
  - So FOSLN can give ASIC the home address as principal place of business (only suburb, state and postcode shown) and the PO Box as the service address. Nothing on ASIC then publishes the home address.
- **Australia Post Business PO Box** needs an ABN (from the #25 decision; not re-checked here). The test is still the same.

## Open points (outside this ticket)

- **Trading as "FOSLN" before registering it.** *Business Names Registration Act 2011* s 18 makes it an offence (30 penalty units) for an entity to carry on business under a name that isn't registered to it, unless, for an individual, the name is their own name. Whether using "FOSLN" as a brand counts as carrying on business under that name before registration is worth a separate check.
- **"The person's name"** in reg 90(1)(d)(i) is the warrantor's name. For an Individual, that is probably the owner's legal name, even after "FOSLN" is registered. This wasn't researched.
